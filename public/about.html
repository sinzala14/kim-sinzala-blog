About Kim Sinzala

Still becoming.

I’m Kim Sinzala, studying Computer Science in Zambia, and I’m deeply interested in where software engineering, artificial intelligence, business, and real-world problem solving meet. For me, technology is not only about writing code—I want to understand why a problem exists, who experiences it, how a system can solve it, whether it can scale, and whether it can become a sustainable venture.

I like turning ideas into working prototypes. Building real systems—such as my work as a developer at TechBack or Access Point—is the fastest way I know to test what I actually understand, and it continually sends me back to mathematics, algorithms, databases, and computer networks with a clear purpose.

Idea → research → prototype → testing → failure → improvement → product.

Opinion

What I Think About Technology

Technology is most interesting when it leaves the screen.

Interesting vs. useful

Building something because it’s technically fun and building it because someone has the problem are different things. The second is harder and worth more.

Local problems

Africa doesn’t need copies of tools built for other places. It needs engineers who understand local infrastructure, money and habits, and build for them.

Software is part of the system

Infrastructure, automation, AI and business models decide whether software matters.
