About

Still becoming.

I’m studying Computer Science, and I’ve become more interested in where software engineering, artificial intelligence, business and real-world problem solving meet. It’s not only about writing code. I want to know why a problem exists, who experiences it, how a system could solve it, whether it can scale, and whether it can become a sustainable business.

I like turning ideas into working prototypes. Building is the fastest way I know to find out what I actually understand, and it usually sends me back to mathematics, algorithms, databases, networks or whatever foundation I’m missing.

Idea → research → prototype → testing → failure → improvement → product.

How I Work

Learn by building

Question the obvious

I ask why a system works the way it does and whether there’s a better approach.

Start practical

Prototypes and experiments over months of designing something untested.

Iterate aggressively

First versions are experiments. I expect to rewrite, improve, sometimes rethink completely.

Think beyond code

Users, operations, economics, infrastructure, security and scalability.

Learn from failure

A failed test or weak idea is useful if it exposes what needs to change.

Opinion

What I Think About Technology

Technology is most interesting when it leaves the screen.

Interesting vs. useful

Building something because it’s technically fun and building it because someone has the problem are different things. The second is harder and worth more.

Local problems

Africa doesn’t need copies of tools built for other places. It needs engineers who understand local infrastructure, money and habits, and build for them.

Software is part of the system

Infrastructure, automation, AI and business models decide whether software matters.
